# What if continuity didn't have to belong to only one predecessor?

Beware that most of this are personal opinions, there are probably a few jokes and most of the tombsonas' descriptions are just copied from the TLT fandom wiki. There are also inconsistencies in things like letter capitalization as different parts of this were written at different points in time.

The fusion/assimilation is two beings becoming one, right? (Rhetorical question)
How for example red and blue mix to create purple. You **can see red** in there, you **can see blue** in there, but you **wouldn’t say it’s either of those**. It’s **purple**, that’s what it is now. More than either of the two could ever be alone. Simple.

The chaos begins when it’s not just colors anymore, **but two conscious beings merging into one**, because intuitively when (many) people try to imagine it, the **thought that one has to be oppressed** in some way appears. Doesn't matter whether it's that "one controls the body of the other", or something else, because that doesn't happen here. This is

**A + B -> C**.

**Not** A + B -> AB (or A+, or B+). The border between **A and B is completely gone**.

I'll add another thought to this. If you can, imagine the **person you were.. I don't know, years ago**. You might say that it's a **different person, but you're still here**, and at the core, you're still you. My point is, **people change all the time**! I'll get back to this in a bit.

But now imagine the assimilation/fusion from a tad different perspective - we still have Conscious being A and Conscious being B, but let's **forget that B is a conscious being** for a moment and view it purely from **A's perspective**. If you simplify it enough to Conscious being **A + B -> C**, you can say that **C is still a continuation of A.. there's just more to them**, but that doesn't mean that A was simply erased. They **just changed some amount**, but it's still them, alive.. in a way.

Now turn it around to **B's perspective. The same thing happens** - Conscious being **B + A -> C**. And **the product is exactly the same in A's and B's perspective**! Meaning both are alive and **the streams of consciousness wasn't interrupted**, they just change and are not really "both" anymore. They're one now. Which, for me personally, is the important part.

Since my memory is so bad, I could already spend my whole life wondering whether I'm really the same "me" I was years ago. I can't prove that I am. Nobody can. But I still experience my life as one continuous story. (I think. You could argue that any moment in time, I am merely a collection of memories, so i couldn't even tell if I wasn't a continuation (and could die any moment and create a new one.))
I don't think of myself in this moment as countless different people who replaced each other over time, *that* would be a horror (as I said already). I think of myself as someone who's constantly changing.

**Fusion, to me, isn't fundamentally different**. It's just change on a scale that humans have never experienced before. And sure, there's still the horror from Animal. (song. a peak one, too. educate yourself if you haven't heard it, but I'm sure you have.) The **fear of losing yourself is real**, and **neither I nor the song are trying to deny that**. But also see that it shifts **from panic toward something more like acceptance** ("I'm finally done concealing, I'm feeling like an animal" and "Or is this evolution, my new solution?").<br>
It's not happy, but it's **no longer fighting the transformation**. It's the moment where fear begins turning into identity. It's the moment where the question "What am I becoming?" changes to a statement, "This is what I am now.".<br>
Which.. still isn't very joyous and whimsical, but not necessarily dreadful and life crushing. Again, I didn't say that I found a way to *entirely not view it as horror*, but to view it **not entirely as horror**.<br>
Fusion/assimilation isn't a fight where one consciousness has to win, because it can't. Just maybe its memories/data can, but I've been trying to **separate consciousness and its data** this entire time.

I also remember that a part of it hit me when i thought of two things

1. you change all the time. time is a linear b!\*\*h (which scares me). you're never the same person you were a second ago. memories, chemistry in your brain, everything changed, even if a tiny bit. now imagine months, years. (which i already said in the text)<br>
2. the body is a machine that can change as well, as long as (in my view) your stream of consciousness stays intact. for example you can start small - implant a magnet in your brain. the body will notice it and eventually make use of it, you'll develop a *feeling*. or you can start big, idc

Now specific questions.<br>
**If A and B fuse, does C remember being A and B, or merely remember their memories?** The memories. But not like "I remember A remembers this". It's much more lower-level. C *remembers* everything. Now it depends whether the brain stored memories with the image of the person (A or B), but let's assume not.<br>
**If C has a thought that neither A nor B would have had, where did that thought "come from"?** Well, from C! The new and independent neural network that has now the synapses of both A and B, which allows for new thoughts.<br>
**If A and B have contradictory memories, does C experience them as contradictory, or do they become one coherent history?** Now this is what I said "let's assume not" to in one of the earlier questions. And it's also one that I don't think I can answer right now. It forces the idea of one consciousness onto separate memories of separate beings, which I understand that you have to force, but I don't wanna do that now.<br>
**If A loved something and B hated it, what does C feel?** Depends on how and why (and more) A loved and B hated it. Again, it's a deep and low-level integration. The networks fuse and it just.. depends, so C may say something like "I love X, but something about it unsettles me...".<br>
**If A and B both feared fusion but C is happy to exist, was the fusion a tragedy, a transformation, or somehow both?** Both. And depends on the view of the person and who is asking that question. To people who knew A and B separately, it's surely weird and probably horrid, maybe ruins the relationship. But it's also the kind of question that I don't know how to answer because it mixes vague things.<br>
**If C sincerely says “I am A,” is that any more or less true than me saying I'm the same person I was years ago?** Isn't! And that's the beautiful part. *Both* "I am A" and "I am B" are true for C to say. But not in the sense that would imply that both A and B are separated by any bit. Another thing that nicely connects it to ordinary human change. C can mourn A or B in the way you can mourn your past selves. Like “I miss being A. I miss being B. But I don't wanna stop being C, and even if I did I can't because time is painfully linear.”<br>
**What does C initially think? What does it feel like to be C?**<br>
Suppose A has always thought “I am A.” and B has always thought “I am B.”. Then after fusion, C wakes up.<br>

Or perhaps the integration itself produces something neither A nor B would recognize.<br>

So now for the important part - The Questions. I'd like to start more simple and continue from there.<br>
As I said, Doc *swaps bodies* instead of finding another host.. *another host*... Given the whole **A + B -> C** idea, that would mean that after the first host, B is already C. Which isn't a problem in any way, just kinda interesting is all. It means that the tombsona as a separate entity doesn't (and can't) leave the host as a separate entity, because there is no separation anymore after the fusion. It's rather the new being, C, who goes and fuses with another host, D, which creates E.<br>

Or maybe they just get transferred into the other, dying body..

But then there's The Problem. Multiple points from this part **point towards** something that the first 1500-ish words of this document try to get rid of - **some boundary between A and B**.<br>
zero_one is perfect in this. The complete rewrite of the host's body makes C sound bit more immortal and the change more permanent, and therefore more like a perfect fusion. And even if the others' host's body dies and they find another one, that's still not a problem, just the C + D -> E that I already talked about.<br>
**The problem is deassimilation** while preserving the host, which Rust apparently does.. but how!? Wouldn't that still assume some boundary between the original A and B? **That boundary is completely gone with no undo button**, since there's only one consciousness now.<br>

**That's where my model starts to fall apart**. But it really does sound like it should work at times, so the problem must be not with my model, but that it's simply not used. At least with Rust, the assimilation is most probably infact *not* perfect fusion, because there's still some kind of information preserved so A (and B) can be reconstructed.

There's a horrifying implication, though. If you assume some boundary between A's and B's ***data*** (not consciousness) and can reconstruct A from C, is the reconstructed A actually A?<br>
Because if you think about it, we already realized that if you look at it from a different perspective, merging isn't the hard part. Reconstruction/division would be. If you divide C into two parts again, A' and B', is A' really A (and B' B)? Same memories, neural structure, therefore personality and everything, but the streams of consciousness merged.. and then split. Wouldn't that make it two Cs now, one with A's brain and one with B's?

Because there aren't just two close streams that you could tear apart anymore (A and B). There's just one.

I'll bring in a color analogy once again. You already **mixed blue and red together**. You can try to split the purple paint in two, **but they're both still purple**. You just have two samples of purple paint now.

And sure, for example zero could maybe make a snapshot of A before the boundary would melt, but I **already assumed that A can be reconstructed**, making the **problem not the reconstruction, but the *stream of consciousness***. Because that **would create a mere *copy* of A, not "revive" A**.

A' will 100% think it's the original, because it is.. physically. It would make no difference to A' or anyone looking at them, not even people who have known them their entire life. It would seem like A is back, except they aren't. **There's only a difference from the perspective of A**, because there isn't any perspective of A anymore.<br>
There's a **difference between the information problem and the continuity problem**.

And I don't actually think that there's anything more to discover, **deassimilation of a fusion should be simply impossible**, so **if Rust or anyone else can** actually deassimilate and have the original host continue as themselves, **then the assimilation cannot have been perfect fusion** as I imagine zero_one's to be, **but rather the infamous**

**A + B -> AB**

where there are two streams of consciousness fighting each other.<br>
